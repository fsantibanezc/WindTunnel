\begin{tcolorbox}[title=Mission Briefing \& Research Objective, colframe=blue!60!black]
In aerodynamics, \textbf{stall} represents the sudden loss of lift when airflow separates from the upper surface of a wing at a critical angle of attack $\alpha_{\text{stall}}$. Using \textbf{Dimensional Analysis}, we can show that the critical stall angle depends on three fundamental dimensionless parameters: airfoil thickness ratio ($t/c$), camber ratio ($m$), and the Reynolds number ($Re$):
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{\text{stall}} = \Phi\left(\frac{t}{c},\, m,\, Re\right) \approx K \cdot \left(\frac{t}{c}\right)^a \cdot \left(1 + m\right)^b \cdot Re^c.
\end{equation}
As an aerospace research consortium, our class will systematically explore this multi-variable relationship using the GPU-accelerated \textbf{WindTunnel lab}. Three specialized student research teams---each dedicated to one governing dimensionless $\Pi$-group---will measure $\alpha_{\text{stall}}$ as a function of their assigned parameter, construct both the linear and log-log scaling plots to extract their power-law exponent ($a$, $b$, or $c$), and collaborate to calibrate and validate a unified empirical stall law.
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{Dimensional Analysis \& The Aerodynamics of Stall}

\subsection{Physical Nature of Aerodynamic Stall}
When an airfoil moves through air at an angle of attack $\alpha$, fluid streams smoothly over both surfaces, generating lift. However, as $\alpha$ increases, air flowing over the upper surface must turn sharply around the nose and decelerate into an increasing pressure hill (known as an \textit{adverse pressure gradient}). At a critical angle called the \textbf{stall angle} ($\alpha_{\text{stall}}$), the sluggish air near the surface can no longer overcome this hill. The flow detaches (separates) from the upper surface, lift reaches its primary attached-flow peak and drops, and the wing stalls.

\subsection{Formulating the Physical Problem with Dimensional Analysis}
Before running expensive wind tunnel tests or computer simulations, aerospace engineers use \textbf{Dimensional Analysis} (the Buckingham $\Pi$ Theorem) to determine which variables truly govern the physics, reducing complex systems of units into universal, dimensionless parameters.

To determine what governs the stall angle $\alpha_{\text{stall}}$, we list every physical variable that could influence the onset of flow separation:

\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Applying the Buckingham \texorpdfstring{$\Pi$}{Pi} Theorem}
We have $n = 7$ physical variables expressed in $k = 3$ fundamental dimensions: mass $[M]$, length $[L]$, and time $[T]$. According to the Buckingham $\Pi$ Theorem, the physical relationship can be expressed by exactly $n - k = 7 - 3 = 4$ independent dimensionless groups ($\Pi$-groups).

Selecting the fluid dynamic reference scale $(\rho,\, U_\infty,\, c)$ as repeating variables, we construct the dimensionless $\Pi$-groups:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Target Dimensionless Output ($\Pi_{\alpha} = \alpha_{\text{stall}}$):} The critical stall angle of attack (dimensionless).
    \item \textbf{Group 1 ($\Pi_1 = t/c$):} The \textbf{Airfoil Thickness Ratio} (maximum thickness relative to chord).
    \item \textbf{Group 2 ($\Pi_2 = h/c = m$):} The \textbf{Airfoil Camber Ratio} (maximum camber relative to chord).
    \item \textbf{Group 3 ($\Pi_3 = \frac{\rho U_\infty c}{\mu} = Re$):} The \textbf{Reynolds Number}, which scales fluid inertia (momentum) relative to viscous friction.
\end{enumerate}

Therefore, dimensional analysis proves that the stall angle is strictly governed by three independent dimensionless groups:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{\alpha_{\text{stall}} = \Phi\left(\Pi_1,\, \Pi_2,\, \Pi_3\right) = \Phi\left(\frac{t}{c},\, m,\, Re\right)}
\end{equation}
These three independent $\Pi$-groups directly define the division of labor across our \textbf{three specialized student research teams}.

\subsection{Physical Intuition Behind the Dimensionless Groups}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Airfoil Thickness ($t/c$):} Controls the roundness of the nose (leading-edge radius $r_{\text{LE}}/c = 1.1019\,(t/c)^2$). A sharp, thin airfoil ($t/c = 0.06$) forces air around an abrupt corner, causing early leading-edge separation. A thicker airfoil ($t/c = 0.18$) softens the leading-edge suction spike, delaying separation to higher angles, until excessive thickness ($t/c = 0.24$) triggers trailing-edge pressure-recovery stall.
    
    \item \textbf{Airfoil Camber ($m$):} Positive camber arches the mean line so the leading edge aligns more smoothly with the incoming airflow at positive incidence, delaying separation to slightly higher angles according to $\alpha_{\text{stall}} \propto (1+m)^b$.
    
    \item \textbf{Reynolds Number ($Re$):} Measures fluid inertia relative to viscous friction. High-$Re$ air has higher near-wall momentum and thinner laminar boundary layers ($\delta/c \propto Re^{-1/2}$), enabling the boundary layer to withstand steeper adverse pressure gradients and delaying stall according to $\alpha_{\text{stall}} \propto Re^c$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Experimental Testing Platform: NCSSM \textit{WindTunnel lab}}

Tests are conducted using NCSSM's \textbf{WindTunnel lab}, a real-time GPU-accelerated numerical wind tunnel powered by the Lattice Boltzmann Method (LBM D2Q9) and Smagorinsky subgrid-scale turbulence modeling.

\subsection{Standardized Data Acquisition (DAQ) Protocol}
To ensure reproducibility across all student research teams, each angle sweep is executed using the \textbf{5-Second Cycle-Averaged DAQ Protocol}:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Geometry \& Flow Configuration:} Enter the 4-digit NACA profile (e.g., \texttt{2412}), set the target Reynolds number ($Re$), and verify that \textit{Smagorinsky LES} is enabled.
    \item \textbf{Angle Dialing \& Flow Settling:} Sweep $\alpha$ using coarse steps ($2.0^\circ$) in the linear attached region and fine steps ($0.5^\circ$) across the stall zone. Wait 3--5 seconds at each angle until the status badge displays \textbf{\textcolor{green!70!black}{Flow Settled $\cdot$ Ready}}.
    \item \textbf{5s Cycle-Averaged Acquisition:} Click \texttt{[Acquire (5s)]} (or press \texttt{L}) to integrate forces over complete vortex-shedding periods and identify the primary attached-flow stall angle $\alpha_{\text{stall}}$ for each profile.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Consortium Research Team Assignments \& Deliverables}

The class is partitioned into \textbf{three specialized research teams}, corresponding directly to the three dimensionless $\Pi$-groups derived from Buckingham $\Pi$ analysis:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Team 1 ($\Pi_1 = t/c$):} Airfoil Thickness Ratio Team
    \item \textbf{Team 2 ($\Pi_2 = m$):} Airfoil Camber Ratio Team
    \item \textbf{Team 3 ($\Pi_3 = Re$):} Reynolds Number Team
\end{itemize}
For each team, the required graphical deliverables are strictly focused on \textbf{two plots}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Main Plot:} Stall angle of attack $\alpha_{\text{stall}}$ as a function of the team's governing parameter (linear scale) with the fitted power-law curve overlaid.
    \item \textbf{Log-Log Plot:} The log-log transformation of that relationship used to extract the power-law exponent via linear regression.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Team 1: Airfoil Thickness Ratio (\texorpdfstring{$\Pi_1 = t/c$}{Pi1 = t/c}) Team}
\textbf{Primary Objective:} Measure $\alpha_{\text{stall}}$ as a function of thickness ratio $t/c$, extract the power-law exponent $a$ in $\alpha_{\text{stall}} \propto (t/c)^a$, and measure the coupled thin-camber validation benchmark \texttt{NACA 4406}.
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Fixed Controls:} Baseline Reynolds number $Re = 1000$, Camber $m = 0.00$ (Symmetric Series).
    \item \textbf{Primary Airfoil Sweep Matrix:} \textbf{\texttt{NACA 0006}} ($t/c = 0.06$), \textbf{\texttt{NACA 0009}} ($t/c = 0.09$), \textbf{\texttt{NACA 0012}} ($t/c = 0.12$), \textbf{\texttt{NACA 0018}} ($t/c = 0.18$), and \textbf{\texttt{NACA 0024}} ($t/c = 0.24$).
    \item \textbf{Team 1 Deliverables (Two-Panel Figure \& Table):}
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Main Plot ($\alpha_{\text{stall}}$ vs.\ $t/c$):} Plot measured stall angle $\alpha_{\text{stall}}$ ($^\circ$) as a function of thickness ratio $t/c$, overlaid with the fitted power-law curve $\alpha_{\text{stall}} = A\,(t/c)^a$.
        \item \textbf{Log-Log Plot ($\ln(\alpha_{\text{stall}})$ vs.\ $\ln(t/c)$):} Plot the log-transformed coordinates for the attached-stall regime ($t/c \in [0.06, 0.18]$) and extract the thickness exponent $a$ from the linear regression slope:
        \begin{equation}
        \ln(\alpha_{\text{stall}}) = a\,\ln\!\left(\frac{t}{c}\right) + \ln A \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \alpha_{\text{stall}} \propto \left(\frac{t}{c}\right)^a,
        \end{equation}
        noting the high-thickness saturation departure at $t/c = 0.24$ (\texttt{NACA 0024}).
    \end{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Coupled Validation Benchmark:} Measure $\alpha_{\text{stall}}$ on \textbf{\texttt{NACA 4406}} ($m=0.04, t/c=0.06, Re=1000$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Team 2: Airfoil Camber Ratio (\texorpdfstring{$\Pi_2 = m$}{Pi2 = m}) Team}
\textbf{Primary Objective:} Measure $\alpha_{\text{stall}}$ as a function of maximum camber ratio $m$, extract the power-law exponent $b$ in $\alpha_{\text{stall}} \propto (1+m)^b$, and measure the coupled thick-camber validation benchmark \texttt{NACA 4418}.
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Fixed Controls:} Baseline Reynolds number $Re = 1000$, Thickness $t/c = 0.12$, Maximum Camber Position $p = 0.4$ (\texttt{NACA X412} Family).
    \item \textbf{Primary Airfoil Sweep Matrix:} \textbf{\texttt{NACA 0012}} ($m = 0.00$), \textbf{\texttt{NACA 1412}} ($m = 0.01$), \textbf{\texttt{NACA 2412}} ($m = 0.02$), \textbf{\texttt{NACA 4412}} ($m = 0.04$), and \textbf{\texttt{NACA 6412}} ($m = 0.06$).
    \item \textbf{Team 2 Deliverables (Two-Panel Figure \& Table):}
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Main Plot ($\alpha_{\text{stall}}$ vs.\ $m$):} Plot measured stall angle $\alpha_{\text{stall}}$ ($^\circ$) as a function of maximum camber ratio $m$, overlaid with the fitted power-law curve $\alpha_{\text{stall}} = B\,(1+m)^b$.
        \item \textbf{Log-Log Plot ($\ln(\alpha_{\text{stall}}/\alpha_{\text{stall},0})$ vs.\ $\ln(1+m)$):} Plot the normalized log-transformed stall angle relative to the symmetric baseline $\alpha_{\text{stall},0}$ (\texttt{NACA 0012}) against $\ln(1+m)$ across $m \in [0.00, 0.06]$, and extract the camber exponent $b$ from the linear regression slope:
        \begin{equation}
        \ln\!\left(\frac{\alpha_{\text{stall}}}{\alpha_{\text{stall},0}}\right) = b\,\ln(1 + m) \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \alpha_{\text{stall}} \propto (1 + m)^b.
        \end{equation}
    \end{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Coupled Validation Benchmark:} Measure $\alpha_{\text{stall}}$ on \textbf{\texttt{NACA 4418}} ($m=0.04, t/c=0.18, Re=1000$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Team 3: Reynolds Number (\texorpdfstring{$\Pi_3 = Re$}{Pi3 = Re}) Team}
\textbf{Primary Objective:} Measure $\alpha_{\text{stall}}$ as a function of Reynolds number $Re$ on \texttt{NACA 2412} and extract the power-law exponent $c$ in $\alpha_{\text{stall}} \propto Re^c$.
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Fixed Controls:} Standard \textbf{\texttt{NACA 2412}} profile ($m = 0.02$, $p = 0.4$, $t/c = 0.12$).
    \item \textbf{Primary Reynolds Number Sweep Matrix:} $Re \in \{300,\, 600,\, 1200,\, 2500,\, 5000\}$.
    \item \textbf{Team 3 Deliverables (Two-Panel Figure \& Table):}
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Main Plot ($\alpha_{\text{stall}}$ vs.\ $Re$):} Plot measured stall angle $\alpha_{\text{stall}}$ ($^\circ$) as a function of Reynolds number $Re$, overlaid with the fitted power-law curve $\alpha_{\text{stall}} = C\,Re^c$.
        \item \textbf{Log-Log Plot ($\ln(\alpha_{\text{stall}})$ vs.\ $\ln(Re)$):} Plot $\ln(\alpha_{\text{stall}})$ against $\ln(Re)$ across all five Reynolds numbers and extract the Reynolds exponent $c$ from the linear regression slope:
        \begin{equation}
        \ln(\alpha_{\text{stall}}) = c\,\ln(Re) + \ln C \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \alpha_{\text{stall}} \propto Re^c.
        \end{equation}
    \end{enumerate}
\end{itemize}

\section{Analysis \& Synthesis Requirements}

Each research team will present its two-panel deliverable figure and data table, followed by the consortium synthesis of the unified stall scaling law:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=4pt]
    \item \textbf{Team 1 Deliverable ($\Pi_1 = t/c$):}
    Present the data table of $(t/c, \alpha_{\text{stall}}, \ln(t/c), \ln(\alpha_{\text{stall}}))$, the main plot of $\alpha_{\text{stall}}$ vs.\ $t/c$, and the log-log plot $\ln(\alpha_{\text{stall}})$ vs.\ $\ln(t/c)$. Report the extracted exponent $a$ and explain why \texttt{NACA 0024} ($t/c = 0.24$) saturates below the thin-to-moderate thickness power-law trend.

    \item \textbf{Team 2 Deliverable ($\Pi_2 = m$):}
    Present the data table of $(m, 1+m, \alpha_{\text{stall}}, \ln(1+m), \ln(\alpha_{\text{stall}}/\alpha_{\text{stall},0}))$, the main plot of $\alpha_{\text{stall}}$ vs.\ $m$, and the log-log plot $\ln(\alpha_{\text{stall}}/\alpha_{\text{stall},0})$ vs.\ $\ln(1+m)$. Report the extracted camber exponent $b$.

    \item \textbf{Team 3 Deliverable ($\Pi_3 = Re$):}
    Present the data table of $(Re, \alpha_{\text{stall}}, \ln(Re), \ln(\alpha_{\text{stall}}))$, the main plot of $\alpha_{\text{stall}}$ vs.\ $Re$, and the log-log plot $\ln(\alpha_{\text{stall}})$ vs.\ $\ln(Re)$. Report the extracted Reynolds number exponent $c$ and relate it to laminar boundary-layer thinning ($\delta/c \propto Re^{-1/2}$).

    \item \textbf{Consortium Synthesis \& Coupled Validation:}
    Combine the three empirically extracted exponents ($a, b, c$) into the unified general stall law:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{\alpha_{\text{stall}}\!\left(m,\, \frac{t}{c},\, Re\right) = K \cdot \left(\frac{t}{c}\right)^a \cdot (1 + m)^b \cdot Re^c},
    \end{equation}
    determine the calibration constant $K$, and validate the formula's predictions ($\alpha_{\text{pred}}$) against the experimentally measured stall angles ($\alpha_{\text{meas}}$) on the two out-of-sample coupled benchmarks \textbf{\texttt{NACA 4406}} ($m = 0.04, t/c = 0.06, Re = 1000$) and \textbf{\texttt{NACA 4418}} ($m = 0.04, t/c = 0.18, Re = 1000$):
    \begin{equation}
    \text{Percent Error} = \left| \frac{\alpha_{\text{pred}} - \alpha_{\text{meas}}}{\alpha_{\text{meas}}} \right| \times 100\%.
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
